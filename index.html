    How it works

    The options

    Platform conversion lift: the platform randomises your audience and counts conversions in both groups for you. The cleanest and least work, if you qualify.

    Platform brand lift: a survey-based test of awareness and similar measures, not sales. Included because it has its own minimum budget.

    Manual holdout: you withhold ads from a random share of your audience and compare. It needs you to control who sees ads, plus enough conversions to see a difference.

    Geo test: you switch ads off in some regions and compare. There are few regions and they differ, so it needs more spend and more care.

    Pause test: you stop ads for a while and watch what happens. It is always possible and always the weakest, because seasonality and everything else changes at the same time.

    The holdout arithmetic

    With T conversions among people who see the ads, a holdout of share h would have about C = T x h / (1 - h) conversions if the ads did nothing. The log of the ratio between the two groups has a standard error of about sqrt(1/T + 1/C). At 95% confidence and 80% power:

    smallest lift seen = exp((1.960 + 0.842) x sqrt(1/T + 1/C)) - 1

    If that is at or below the lift you care about, the holdout is yes. Up to one and a half times it, underpowered. Beyond that, no. For a fuller plan, use Holdout Sizer.

    Read this before you plan around the answer

    The thresholds are the weak part. Most come from guides that cite the platform, not from the platform. Treat "meets the reported thresholds" as "worth checking", not as a promise.

    A test you cannot run is not a reason to trust the dashboard. If nothing is available yet, the fix list says what would open one up.

    The holdout figure assumes a clean split. Leakage, repeat buyers and seasonality all make real tests noisier.
